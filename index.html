Our Story

Long Before Beyond Had a Name

When my oldest daughter was four, her pre-K teacher encouraged me to seek an autism evaluation. A diagnosis followed. I remember feeling lost and scared, hearing what she might never do when what I needed most was help seeing what was possible.

Long before I knew the language of ABA, I was already learning something important as her mother: meaningful learning does not have to look the same for every child. Our dining room became a learning space, but so did play, daily routines, conversations, choices, and the moments that naturally happened throughout our day.

I never believed a child should have to sit at a table simply because that is where learning is expected to happen. What matters is whether the child is engaged, learning useful skills, and making progress toward meaningful, individualized goals. Sometimes structured teaching may be helpful. Other times, the best learning happens while moving, playing, communicating, completing a routine, or participating in everyday life.

Years later, my professional work in ABA gave me a deeper understanding of the principles behind many of the things I had learned through experience. Beyond Adventures & Growth™ grew from both perspectives—the parent who wanted her child to be understood and the professional who believes support should be individualized, practical, respectful, and connected to real life.

I created Beyond because I have never forgotten the uncertainty families can feel when they are looking for answers. I want families to feel heard, children to feel valued for who they are, and parents to know they do not have to figure everything out alone.

Services

How We Support Children & Families

Beyond Adventures & Growth™ is preparing to provide individualized, in-home Applied Behavior Analysis (ABA) services. Our approach focuses on meaningful skill acquisition and functional goals that matter in everyday life—communication, independence, participation, daily routines, and other priorities identified with the child and family.

Learning does not have to happen at a table.

ABA can be taught through play, movement, preferred activities, daily routines, and real-life experiences when those settings fit the child and the goal. Structured or table-based teaching may be useful for some skills, but it is not the only way to learn. Our focus is not on making a child sit through repetitive material simply because it is traditional. The focus is on engagement, useful learning, progress toward individualized goals, and helping skills carry over into everyday life.

FAQ

Questions You May Be Asking

Starting something new can bring a lot of questions. You don’t have to figure it all out at once—we’re here to help you understand your next steps.

How do I get started?

Start with our Get Support page for contact options and an overview of the next steps. You do not need to have everything figured out before asking a general question. Beyond is preparing to launch; reaching out does not confirm enrollment or a service start date.

Where will services take place?

Beyond plans to begin with in-home ABA services in New Jersey. A center is not currently established. Service areas and availability will be confirmed as staffing and launch arrangements are finalized.

What happens during the first conversation?

Our planned first conversation is a chance to listen: what matters to your family, what has helped, and what you hope support could look like. We can then discuss availability and possible next steps. For now, please keep email inquiries general and do not send medical records or other sensitive health information.

How can I check insurance coverage?

Visit our Insurance & Payment page for current information. Accepted plans have not yet been confirmed. You can also contact your insurer to ask about your benefits and any referral or authorization requirements; coverage must be verified before services begin.

Will ABA change my child’s personality?

The purpose of ethical, individualized ABA should not be to erase personality, identity, harmless differences, interests, or individuality. Goals should address meaningful skills and barriers related to communication, safety, participation, autonomy, learning, relationships, independence, and quality of life.

How will I be involved?

Caregiver involvement depends on the person’s age, service model, living situation, treatment goals, clinical recommendations, and funding requirements. Caregiver education is intended to build understanding and confidence — not blame or evaluate families.

Can Beyond work with a school?

School collaboration or school-based services may be available when clinically appropriate and when required consent, authorization, funding, school approval, and operational arrangements are in place.

Is an autism diagnosis required?

Insurance-based ABA services may require an autism diagnosis and a prescription or referral depending on the payer and applicable requirements. Beyond may also develop supports that are not limited to one diagnosis, subject to scope, licensing, funding, and program availability.

How many hours will be recommended?

A responsible recommendation cannot be made before an appropriate assessment. Recommendations depend on individual needs, goals, functioning, environment, participation, medical necessity when applicable, and funding requirements.

How are staff selected and prepared?

Beyond considers experience, training, communication style, schedule, clinical needs, environmental demands, and overall fit. Staff receive orientation, plan-specific preparation, supervision, and ongoing support according to their role.

Does Beyond use table-based teaching?

Structured teaching may be used when clinically appropriate. Beyond also emphasizes naturalistic teaching because skills need to be useful during everyday routines and in real environments.

Does Beyond serve adults?

Beyond is being developed with a lifespan vision. Launch populations and services will depend on clinical appropriateness, licensing, staffing, funding, and current program capacity.
