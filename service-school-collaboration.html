School Collaboration

Helping the adults around a child share useful information.

What collaboration can look like

Children may need different supports at school and at home. With appropriate consent, the clinical team can exchange relevant observations with school professionals, discuss communication and daily routines, and clarify each team’s responsibilities.

  • Sharing the child’s strengths, interests, and ways of communicating.
  • Discussing relevant progress or patterns across settings.
  • Coordinating compatible supports when appropriate, rather than assuming the same strategy fits every setting.
  • Helping families prepare questions and summarize observations for a meeting.

An everyday example

A child uses a visual routine at home, while school transitions remain difficult. With family permission and school agreement, the teams can compare observations and discuss whether a familiar visual support fits the school context. The school team makes its educational decisions through its own process.

Understanding school support in New Jersey

An Individualized Education Program (IEP) describes special education and related services for an eligible student. Parents participate in its development. A parent can make a written referral to the district for a special-education evaluation; eligibility is determined through the district’s evaluation process.

The NJDOE parent page links to PRISE, which explains special-education rights, and information about SPAN and district Special Education Parent Advisory Groups (SEPAGs). These resources can help families learn about the process and connect with support.

Beyond’s role

Any school collaboration depends on consent, school agreement, service scope, and availability. Beyond does not replace the Child Study Team, provide legal representation, decide eligibility or placement, or guarantee school-based services. Clinical and educational roles remain distinct.
