Community-Based Learning

Making room for participation, choice, and belonging beyond home.

What this means

Community learning focuses on using meaningful skills and supports in places a child and family want to visit. It may involve a library, park, recreation activity, or shop. Being present is not the only goal: comfort, communication, access, and the child’s choices matter too.

What planning may include

  • Choosing an outing that matters to the child and family.
  • Considering noise, crowds, accessibility, timing, and opportunities for a break.
  • Practicing a relevant skill in a familiar setting before trying it elsewhere.
  • Planning communication, adult support, and safety arrangements.
  • Reviewing the experience and changing the plan when needed.

An everyday example

A family would like to visit the library. Preparation might include looking at pictures of the space and identifying how the child can request a break. An initial visit could be brief, with the option to leave. Participation is adjusted to the child rather than measured by how long they tolerate discomfort.

How Beyond can support your family

Beyond’s initial focus is in-home ABA. Goals may include preparation for everyday outings and discussion of supports with caregivers. Direct community sessions require separate confirmation of clinical suitability, staffing, permission, availability, and funding authorization where applicable. Transportation, supervision at events, and camp services are not automatically included.

For possible NJ summer-camp financial assistance and one-to-one aide support, eligible families can explore PerformCare’s community and camp resources. Funding and availability are limited; this is a separate program.
